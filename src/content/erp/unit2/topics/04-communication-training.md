# Communication & Training

## Why communication is the key

Recall the QnA: *"To handle fear and uncertainty — for this, **communication is the key**."* And the chapter summary: *"**Communication is quite important.**"*

## The six objectives of communication

> [!EXAM]
> **Communication can have different objectives:**
>
> 1. **Make the entire organization aware of the project**
> 2. **Inform project stakeholders of the regular status of the project**
> 3. **Congratulate people as soon as one project milestone is achieved**
> 4. **Make people aware of what new is going to come** — new processes, new roles
> 5. **Inform the core team members much before the project ends what will be their new role**
> 6. **Senior management to communicate to employees that "ERP implementation is not to take out their jobs"** but to help them do something **more interesting and more value adding** for the organization

> [!INTUITION]
> The six objectives answer the resistance questions **one for one**, which is the cleanest way to remember them:
>
> | Objective | Answers the fear |
> |---|---|
> | 1–2 — awareness, status | *"What is happening and where are we?"* |
> | 3 — congratulate on milestones | Sustains momentum through a **long timeline** |
> | 4 — what is coming | *"What will be my new role? More work?"* |
> | 5 — core team's next role | *"Do I need to go back to my old place?"* |
> | 6 — **not to take out their jobs** | *"Will my job remain?"* |
>
> **Objective 6 is the heaviest and it is deliberately assigned to senior management**, not to the project team. Only someone with the authority to *make* that promise can credibly *give* it — a project manager saying "your jobs are safe" carries no weight.
>
> Note the phrasing is not merely reassurance: the promise is that people will do **"something more interesting and more value adding."** That is objective 6 doing double duty — it removes the Level-1 fear *and* offers the Level-2 payoff.

> [!TRAP]
> **Objective 3 — "congratulate people as soon as one project milestone is achieved" — looks like the trivial one and is not.**
>
> Unit 1 listed **"long timeline"** as an implementation challenge, with the specific difficulty being *"difficult to sustain momentum"* and *"benefits/results cannot be delayed beyond a point."* An ERP project runs for years with **nothing visible to show** until go-live.
>
> Milestone celebrations are **manufactured short-term feedback** in a project that provides none naturally. It is the same instinct as BPR's *"show early success within first three to six months."*

## The communication strategy

> [!EXAM]
> **A communication strategy can have different elements:**
>
> - **Identify the right audience for communication**
> - **Methods and vehicles for communication according to the audience**
> - **Determine communication frequency & time frame**
> - **Who should be communicated what:**
>   - **Core team**
>   - **Project team**
>   - **Stakeholders**
>   - **End users**
> - **Determine communicators**

> [!INTUITION]
> The five elements are the standard journalistic set, and naming them that way makes the list impossible to forget:
>
> $$\textbf{Who} \;(\text{audience}) \;\cdot\; \textbf{How} \;(\text{methods}) \;\cdot\; \textbf{When} \;(\text{frequency}) \;\cdot\; \textbf{What} \;(\text{content per audience}) \;\cdot\; \textbf{By whom} \;(\text{communicators})$$
>
> The two that carry the real content are **"who should be communicated what"** and **"determine communicators."**
>
> The first says the **same message must not go to everyone** — the four audiences have genuinely different concerns. The core team needs detail; end users need to know what changes on their screen; stakeholders need status against plan.
>
> The second says **the sender matters as much as the message**, which is objective 6 again: reassurance about jobs must come from senior management, while a technical change is better explained by a power user.

> [!EXAM]
> **The four audiences — Core team · Project team · Stakeholders · End users.** Note that **core team and project team are listed separately**: the core team are the business experts seconded onto the project (who face the *"back to my old place"* problem), while the project team is the wider implementation group.

## Training

> [!EXAM]
> **Training addresses:**
> - **Unknown aspects, fear of ignorance**
> - **"How it can help me"**

> [!TRAP]
> Read that carefully — **the stated purpose of training is not competence, it is fear.**
>
> The deck's two bullets are *"fear of ignorance"* and *"how it can help me"*. Of course training also teaches people to use the system, but in a **change management** chapter it is listed as a **fear-reduction instrument**, sitting under *"First Level — Handle fear and uncertainty."*
>
> That reframing explains the second bullet, which would otherwise be odd in a training syllabus: **"how it can help me"** is the WIIFM question. Good ERP training does not only teach the transaction — it shows the user **what they personally get** from it.

> [!EXAM]
> **Training also appears elsewhere in the course, and the distinctions are examinable:**
>
> | Training | Phase | Audience |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Core team training** | **Business Blueprinting** | the business experts designing the To-Be |
> | **End user training** | **Final Preparation & Go Live** | everyone who will use the system |
> | **Change management training** | **Business Blueprinting** *(see next topic)* | the change team itself |
> | **Orientation training** | **Project Preparation** | senior & key people |
>
> Four different trainings, four different phases, four different audiences. **Unit 1's MCQ bank tests the first two against each other.**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Training is also a direct cost** — 45 lakhs in Unit 1's sample cost table, with the note *"multi-locations, travel."* And it appears in the **hidden costs** list, because organisations consistently underestimate it.
>
> The reason it is underestimated is visible in this chapter: budgets are set for **teaching the software**, but the change-management purpose — repeated reassurance, WIIFM, champions demonstrating benefits — requires **far more contact time than a functional syllabus implies.**
