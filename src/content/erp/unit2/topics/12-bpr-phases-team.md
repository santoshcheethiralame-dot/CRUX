# BPR Phases & the Reengineering Team

## The phases

> [!EXAM]
> **BPR — Phases:**
>
> - **Selection of Process to be redesigned**
> - **Have the Reengineering team in place**
> - **Process Diagnosis**
> - **Process Redesign**
> - **Prototyping**
> - **Full Scale Implementation**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — "Select The Doctor, Redesign, Prototype, Implement":**
>
> | Word | Phase |
> |---|---|
> | **S**elect | **Selection** of the process |
> | **T**he | **Team** in place |
> | **D**octor | **Diagnosis** |
> | **R**edesign | **Redesign** |
> | **P**rototype | **Prototyping** |
> | **I**mplement | **Full scale implementation** |
>
> The medical framing is genuinely apt and worth carrying: you **choose the patient**, assemble the **team**, **diagnose** before prescribing, design the **treatment**, **trial** it on a few cases, and only then **roll it out**.
>
> **Diagnosis before redesign is the non-negotiable order** — prescribing before diagnosing is exactly failure reason 5, *"little change in process, not redesign."*

> [!TRAP]
> Note that **prototyping sits between redesign and implementation** — it is a distinct phase, not an optional extra.
>
> This is the direct answer to failure reason 7, *"large scale implementation without prototyping"*, and to key-to-success #2, *"prototype every process before large scale implementation."*

## The team

> [!EXAM]
> **The Reengineering Team structure:**
>
> **Steering Committee · Leader · Process Owner · Reengineering Team · Reengineering Coordinator · Change Coordinator**
>
> The deck's note: **"BPR project is a management driven project and typically has some skill mix."**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — six roles as three pairs:**
>
> | Pair | Roles | Job |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Two leaders** | **Leader** · **Steering Committee** | authority and arbitration |
> | **Two doers** | **Process Owner** · **Reengineering Team** | own it and redesign it |
> | **Two coordinators** | **Reengineering Coordinator** · **Change Coordinator** | the *hard* side and the *soft* side |
>
> **2 leaders, 2 doers, 2 coordinators.** The coordinator pair is the elegant part: one coordinates the **technical** work, the other coordinates the **human** work — the deck calls them the *"hard"* part and the change management programme respectively.

### 1 — Leader

> [!EXAM]
> - **Identifies the key business processes of the organization**
> - **Appoints senior managers as Process owners**
> - **Creates environment by motivating & supporting the team**
> - **Establishes measures of performance and rewards**
> - **Articulates and communicates the need to reengineer**
>
> *The deck's note: **the Leader is the Project Manager**.*

> [!INTUITION]
> Two of the Leader's five duties are **not project-management duties at all** — *establishes measures of performance and rewards*, and *articulates and communicates the need to reengineer*.
>
> These are **change management responsibilities**, and they map onto Chapter 4's *organizational design* (measures and incentives) and *communication* (the need for change). **The Leader is the point where the BPR project and the change programme meet.**

### 2 — Steering Committee

> [!EXAM]
> - **Settle issues outside the scope of individual processes**
> - **Determine order of priority**
> - **Allocation of resources**
> - **Settlement of inter-process disputes**

> [!TRAP]
> **Three of the four duties are about resolving conflict** — issues outside a single process, priority order, and inter-process disputes.
>
> That is because processes **cross departments**, so a redesign of one process changes work in departments that report elsewhere. When Order Management's redesign creates extra work for Quality, no process owner can settle it. **The steering committee exists for exactly the disputes that have no other forum.**
>
> This is the same body, with the same purpose, as Unit 1's steering committee — *"senior, more managerial"* — formed in **Pre-Implementation**.

### 3 — Process Owner

> [!EXAM]
> - **Appoints the reengineering team for the process**
> - **Obtains resources for the team**
> - **Motivates, inspires and advises the team**
> - **Supports and protects radically new ideas**
> - **Manages the Process once reengineering is over**
>
> *The deck's note: **Process Owner is the technical subject matter expert** of the processes involved.*

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Supports and protects radically new ideas"** is an unusual thing to write into a job description, and it is deliberate.
>
> A radical proposal is fragile — it is easy to object to, since it has no track record and threatens established ways of working. Somebody senior has to **shield it long enough to be evaluated**, or the organisation will regress to incremental tinkering, which is failure reason 5.
>
> Note also the last duty: **"manages the Process once reengineering is over."** The Process Owner is **permanent**, unlike the reengineering team. This directly addresses a common process problem the diagnosis phase names: *"clear ownership of process missing"* and *"no end-to-end ownership."* **BPR does not just redesign the process; it installs an owner for it.**

### 4 — Reengineering Team

> [!EXAM]
> - **Produce ideas and plans and debate them exhaustively**
> - **Redesign the Process**
> - **Sell the designed process to the people**
> - **Continue through to implementation**

> [!TRAP]
> **"Sell the designed process to the people"** is a design-team responsibility here, not a separate communications function.
>
> The team that designed it must persuade the people who will use it — because they are the only ones who can explain *why* it is designed that way. And **"continue through to implementation"** prevents the classic handover failure where designers produce a document and disappear, leaving implementers with a design they do not understand.

### 5 — Reengineering Coordinator

> [!EXAM]
> - **Advise the leader on overall management of the "hard" part**
> - **Coordinate the ongoing reengineering activities**
> - **Provide common approaches**
> - **Help in selecting insiders and outsiders for teams**
> - **Monitor the Process owner**
> - **Anticipate and develop infrastructure for the effort**

### 6 — Change Coordinator

> [!EXAM]
> - **Helps the leader fashion the change management programme**
> - **Follows up on the implementation of the programme**
> - **Tests and reports periodically the reactions of the rest of the folks**
> - **Conscience keeper of the leader**

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Conscience keeper of the leader"** is the most memorable phrase in the chapter and worth quoting in an answer.
>
> The Leader is driving toward *radical* and *dramatic* outcomes, and is therefore the person most likely to push past what the organisation can absorb. The Change Coordinator's job is to **say so** — to be the voice that reports how people are actually reacting rather than how the plan says they should be.
>
> **"Tests and reports periodically the reactions of the rest of the folks"** is Chapter 4's **Assessment** process — *"uncovering and prioritizing risks"*, *"identify and prioritize handling of perception"* — appearing here as a BPR role.
>
> **Multiple roles across both chapters exist to make sure somebody's job is to notice that people are unhappy.** That redundancy is not accidental.

> [!EXAM]
> The deck notes that **roles could be merged and played by fewer individuals** — the same caution it gives about the ASAP change management chart. **Six roles does not mean six people; it means six responsibilities that must each have an owner.**

## How the roles relate

> [!EXAM]
> **BPR Team Relationships** — the deck's diagram:
>
> - The **Leader** *appoints* **Process Owners**, and is *advised and informed* by both the **Reengineering Coordinator** and the **Change Coordinator**
> - The **Steering Committee** *advises* the Leader
> - Each **Process Owner** *leads* and *appoints* a **Redesign Team**
> - The **Reengineering Coordinator** *coordinates* across the redesign teams
> - **Consultants** attach to the redesign teams *(drawn as broken ovals)*

> [!INTUITION]
> Three structural points from the diagram:
>
> **The two coordinators advise but do not command.** They sit beside the Leader, not above the teams — consistent with the Change Coordinator being a *conscience keeper* rather than a manager.
>
> **There is one Process Owner and one Redesign Team per process**, so a BPR programme touching four processes has four of each, with the Reengineering Coordinator providing *"common approaches"* so they do not diverge.
>
> **Consultants are drawn as broken ovals** — the deck notes *"most of the times BPR is facilitated by consultants"*, and that **"generally a matrix organization is preferred"**, with the steering committee holding **indirect** control over the rest of the team.

> [!EXAM]
> On why outsiders are needed at all, the deck is direct:
>
> > **"Insiders are always 'biased' by virtue of being in the system for long. So external involvement is always beneficial."**
>
> This is listed among the **key inputs to redesign** alongside *stated principles*, *organization mission, vision, values & culture*, and *the possibility of a prototype and its results*.
