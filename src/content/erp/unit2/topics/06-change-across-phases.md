# Change Management Across the ERP Phases

## When does change management happen?

> [!EXAM]
> The deck poses it as a question:
>
> > **When do the change management actions happen? Beginning? End? Somewhere in-between? All throughout?**
>
> **The answer is: all throughout.** The chapter then lists specific activities for four stages of the project.

> [!INTUITION]
> The question is rhetorical but the wrong answers are the instructive ones.
>
> **"Beginning"** is the kickoff-meeting theory of change management — announce it once and assume the message holds. **"End"** is worse: training people the week before go-live, when the design decisions that affect their jobs were made a year earlier and cannot be revisited.
>
> Recall the ASAP grid contrast from Unit 1: **Ascendant is a grid, not a sequence**, with six workstreams — including **Organization** — running across every phase. **Change management is a workstream, not a phase.**

## During Project Preparation

> [!EXAM]
> - **Develop change management plan**
> - **Identify change sponsors** — *for all locations: each factory, each office where ERP is going to be implemented*
> - **Assign people to core change team roles**
> - **Create extended change team**
> - **Change management training planning**

> [!TRAP]
> **"Identify change sponsors for all locations"** is the item worth noticing.
>
> A single corporate sponsor is not enough — you need a sponsor **at every site**. That is because change is experienced locally: the plant manager in one city has credibility there that a head-office executive does not.
>
> This is the same reasoning behind the **Key Site Sponsors** on the ASAP role chart, and it is why the **Rollout** deployment strategy pairs naturally with change management — staged locations mean sponsors can be developed one wave at a time.

## During Business Blueprinting

> [!EXAM]
> **Change communication framework:**
> - **Identify point person to manage project communications**
> - **Identify methods and ways for communication**
> - **Developing communication content**
> - **Identify frequency of communication to different stakeholders**
>
> **Conduct change management training:**
> - **Develop organizational change management training strategy**
> - **Select training partner and conduct training**
> - **Evaluating training effectiveness**

> [!INTUITION]
> Those first four bullets **are the communication strategy** from the earlier topic — audience, methods, content, frequency, communicator — now placed in a specific phase.
>
> **Blueprinting is the right home for it**, because this is the phase where the **To-Be process** is designed. That is the moment the organisation first learns concretely **what will change about people's jobs** — so it is the moment the communication machinery must already be running.
>
> Note **"evaluating training effectiveness"** — training is not merely delivered, it is **measured**. That connects to the Gartner finding that *70% of failed projects did not use performance metrics.*

## During Realization

> [!EXAM]
> **Main objective at this stage is to facilitate implementation and go live by keeping people involved, focused and motivated.**
>
> - **Identify & implement appropriate action to address key concerns**
> - **Sustain sponsorship & communications strategies**
> - **Maintain awareness of / implement skills development**
> - **Knowledge transfer strategies**
> - **Team Building** — *helps to nurture and strengthen relationships among project members, promote communication, cooperation and trust*

> [!TRAP]
> The operative word is **"sustain"** — *sustain sponsorship and communications strategies.*
>
> Realization is the **long middle** of the project: configuration and testing, months of work with nothing visible to the business. This is exactly where **communication decays** — the kickoff excitement is gone and go-live is not yet near.
>
> It is also where Unit 1's challenge *"long timeline — difficult to sustain momentum"* bites hardest, and why **milestone congratulation** is a listed communication objective.
>
> Note that **team building** here is aimed **inward at the project team**, not at end users. After a year of pressure, the team that must deliver go-live needs its own maintenance.

## During Go Live and Project Closing

> [!EXAM]
> - **Summarizing lessons learned is an important activity during project closing.** Many of these lessons will be **useful to the organization post-implementation**
> - **Change Team resolves any open issues**
> - **Address the impact of withdrawal of legacy systems**
> - **Disbands the core and extended Change Teams, and recognizes team members as appropriate**

> [!EXAM]
> The deck flags this phase as the critical one:
>
> > **"The go-live phase is critical from a change management perspective. Some people may literally feel helpless if applications they are used to is withdrawn."**

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Address the impact of withdrawal of legacy systems"** is the most human item in the chapter and the one most often overlooked.
>
> The project frames go-live as **gaining** a new system. For the person doing the job, it is **losing** the tool they have used for fifteen years — and with it, the fluency that made them good at their job. A competent, confident employee becomes a beginner overnight.
>
> That is why the word is **"helpless"** rather than "inconvenienced", and why the two Level-1 measures pointed here — the **24×7 help desk** and the **parallel run of the old system** — matter more than any amount of prior messaging. **The parallel run from Unit 1 is a change management instrument as much as a technical safeguard.**

> [!EXAM]
> **"Disbands the core and extended Change Teams, and recognizes team members as appropriate"** closes the loop on two earlier threads:
>
> - Unit 1's people-challenge **"creation, disbanding, relocating"** of large teams
> - The resistance question **"do I need to go back to my old place after the project?"**
>
> **Recognition at disbanding is the answer to *"will my new skill be valued?"*** — and it is the last chance to give it.

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Summarizing lessons learned"** is the same activity as Unit 1's implementation-closure step: *create a small team to **harvest** knowledge, re-usable parts and marketing materials.*
>
> Both chapters place it at closure, and for the same reason — this is the only moment when the experience is still fresh **and** the people who had it are still assembled. A week later they are dispersed and the knowledge is gone.

## The four stages at a glance

> [!EXAM]
> | Phase | Change management focus |
> |---|---|
> | **Project Preparation** | **Set up** — plan, sponsors, core & extended teams, training plan |
> | **Business Blueprinting** | **Communicate & train** — communication framework, change management training |
> | **Realization** | **Sustain** — keep people involved, focused and motivated; team building |
> | **Go Live & Closing** | **Support & close** — open issues, legacy withdrawal, lessons learned, disband and recognise |
>
> **Set up → Communicate → Sustain → Close.** Note there is **no change activity listed for Pre-Implementation**, because the change team is created *during* Project Preparation — consistent with Unit 1, where Phase 1 ends before the project is committed.
