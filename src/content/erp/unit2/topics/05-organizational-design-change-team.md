# Organizational Design & the Change Team

## The cascade from technology to structure

> [!EXAM]
> **New technology (ERP adoption) will have an impact on the structure of the organization:**
>
> - **New processes lead to new roles and responsibilities and a new organization structure**
> - **Process changes feed changes to roles and responsibilities**
> - **New job descriptions are written. New reporting structures are defined.**
> - **New performance measures are developed** that decide **what behaviour the organization will promote and how to reward it**. **New incentive systems are designed.**

> [!DERIVE]
> **The cascade runs in one direction and each step forces the next:**
>
> $$\textbf{New technology} \rightarrow \textbf{new processes} \rightarrow \textbf{new roles} \rightarrow \textbf{new structure} \rightarrow \textbf{new measures \& incentives}$$
>
> This is the **People–Process–Technology triangle** in motion. You changed the Technology corner; the Process corner had to follow because the ERP embeds its own best-practice processes; and the People corner follows from that, because a process executed by different hands in a different order **is a different set of jobs**.
>
> **The last step is the one organisations stop short of.** Redrawing the org chart is visible and gets done. Rewriting **performance measures and incentives** is invisible, politically awkward, and usually deferred — which leaves employees measured on the old process while being asked to follow the new one.

## What organizational design means

> [!EXAM]
> **Organizational Design means:**
> - **New / defined Roles and Responsibilities**
> - **New / defined Reporting Structure**
> - **Job Descriptions**
> - **New / defined Performance Measures and Incentive systems** — which **can play an enormous role in determining and reinforcing desired behaviours**
>
> **The basic objective of Organizational Design is:**
>
> > **"The architecture of the organization needs to be aligned with the new process and technology."**

> [!INTUITION]
> That one-sentence objective is worth memorising verbatim — it is the chapter's definition and a likely exam quote.
>
> The word **"architecture"** is well chosen. An organisation has a structure the way a building does, and you cannot install a new process into an incompatible structure any more than you can add a floor to a building not designed to carry it. **Alignment is structural, not attitudinal.**
>
> Note also **"reinforcing desired behaviours."** Incentives do not merely reward — they **tell people what the organization actually values**, as opposed to what it says in the kickoff meeting. If the ERP go-live is a stated priority but nobody's appraisal mentions it, employees will correctly conclude it is not a priority.

## The Change Team

> [!EXAM]
> **The organizational change management strategy includes creation of the core and extended Change Team.**
>
> | | **Core Change Team** | **Extended Change Team** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **What it does** | **Implements organizational change management steps** | **Introduces and conducts risk assessments, communicates about the project, implements action plans** |
> | **Reports to** | **Project Sponsor and Manager** | — |
> | **Who** | **Dedicated resources from HR, OD professionals and/or consulting team** | **Part-time members from line management or consulting team** |
> | **Composition note** | **Ideally a mix of long and short-term employees**, bringing a **wide range of company experience** | — |

> [!EXAM]
> **The Change Team Lead:**
> - **Must know the organization well and be well-respected within it**
> - **Will become the symbolic face of the organizational change management program**
> - **Therefore, the more credibility he/she holds in the organization the better**

> [!TRAP]
> **The change teams are generally *not* part of the primary implementation team.** The deck states this twice:
>
> > *"Note: generally these teams are not part of the primary implementation team. These are more of organization change agents — track the expected change in new settings; report progress, escalate timely."*
>
> This is a genuine separation of concerns and a likely exam point. The **implementation team builds the system**; the **change team prepares the organisation**. Merging them means change management gets squeezed out whenever the build falls behind — which it will, since Unit 1 records schedule overruns averaging 230%.

> [!INTUITION]
> Three details in the composition repay attention:
>
> **"Dedicated resources from HR, OD professionals"** — the core team is staffed from **HR and organizational development**, not from IT. This is not a technical role.
>
> **"A mix of long and short-term employees"** — long-serving staff know where the resistance will come from and who must be persuaded; newer staff are not invested in the current way of working. **You need both the institutional memory and the outside eye.**
>
> **The lead's qualification is credibility, not expertise** — *"well-respected"*, *"symbolic face"*. Since the whole job is persuasion, the person's standing **is** the qualification.

## The ASAP change management role chart

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's organization chart for change management roles under **ASAP**:
>
> **Project sponsor** → **Steering Committee** → **Project Manager** → **Core Change Team** → **Extended Change Team** → **Key Site Sponsors** and **Line Managers**, with the **Change Team Lead** heading the change teams.
>
> The core change team carries six named **"points"** — one per ASAP change process:
>
> **Knowledge Transfer Point · Risk Assessment Point · Skills Development Point · Communications Point · Sponsorship Point · Organizational Optimization Point** — plus a **Project Risk Manager**.

> [!TRAP]
> The deck's own caution about that chart is important:
>
> > *"Note that the org chart above does not mean having a dedicated team. These are more like **shared duties and roles**. At times, role responsibilities related to change management look trivial, but in reality it is not easy to achieve."*
>
> **Six "points" does not mean six people.** In a mid-sized project one person may hold three of them. What the chart guarantees is that **each of the six ASAP processes has a named owner** — the same principle as the RACI idea that every responsibility must be somebody's.
>
> The six points are exactly the **six ASAP change management processes** of the topic after next, so the chart is really the methodology drawn as an org chart.

> [!EXAM]
> Note the connection to Unit 1: **the steering committee and project sponsor** already appeared in **Pre-Implementation** as *"senior, more managerial"* bodies. Here they reappear at the top of the change chain — because **change management authority has to come from the same place project authority does.**
