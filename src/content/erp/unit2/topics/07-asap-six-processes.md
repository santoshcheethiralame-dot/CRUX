# ASAP's Six Change Management Processes

## The six processes

> [!EXAM]
> **Organizational Change Management — the ASAP way. Six change management processes**, each with its one-line definition:
>
> | Process | Definition |
> |---|---|
> | **Assessment** | **Uncovering and prioritizing risks** |
> | **Communications** | **Designing and delivering key messages** |
> | **Sponsorship** | **Gaining & demonstrating leadership support** |
> | **Organizational Optimization** | **Aligning jobs and processes with the new business environment** |
> | **Skills Development** | **Organizational change management training** |
> | **Knowledge Transfer** | **Capturing and sharing lessons learned** |

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — the six spell A SOCKS:**
>
> | Letter | Process |
> |---|---|
> | **A** | **Assessment** |
> | **S** | **Sponsorship** |
> | **O** | **Organizational optimization** |
> | **C** | **Communications** |
> | **K** | **Knowledge transfer** |
> | **S** | **Skills development** |
>
> *Change management wears **A SOCKS**.* Silly, and that is why it sticks — six unconnected nouns are otherwise very hard to recall under exam pressure.
>
> They also pair up naturally: **Assessment and Sponsorship** are the *set-up* pair (find the risks, get the backing); **Communications and Skills development** are the *outward* pair (tell people, train people); **Knowledge transfer and Organizational optimization** are the *embed* pair (keep the learning, change the structure).

## Core vs supporting

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's diagram splits the six into **Core** and **Supporting** processes, with **Communications** shown spanning across the others.
>
> > **"Communication spans across the other 5. But has its own focus as well."**

> [!TRAP]
> **Communications is both one of the six and the medium for the other five** — that dual role is the examinable subtlety.
>
> Every other process **produces something that must be communicated**: an assessment produces risks people must hear about; sponsorship is worthless unless it is *visible*; skills development must be announced before it can be attended. So communications runs through all of them.
>
> But it *"has its own focus as well"* — the communication strategy, framework, frequency and channels are work in their own right, not a by-product. **If it were only a medium it would not need a point person; the deck assigns one.**

## The six in detail

> [!EXAM]
> **Assessment** — **identify and prioritize handling of perception**
>
> **Sponsorship** — **ensure the messages and support from top management (sponsors) is visible & timely.** **Not just lip-service**
>
> **Communication** — **regular · different mediums · customized to stakeholders · framework of communication**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Assessment is about "perception", not about facts** — that is the word the deck uses, and it is the right one.
>
> The risk being assessed is not *"will the server fail"* but *"which groups believe this project threatens them, and how strongly."* It is **risk assessment applied to people**, running the same six-step shape as Unit 1's risk management: identify → analyse impact → assess probability → **prioritize** → mitigate → contingency.
>
> **"Not just lip-service"** under Sponsorship is the deck being blunt. An executive who endorses the project in a launch email and is never seen again has given *lip service*; the requirement is **visible and timely** support — showing up when a decision is contested, which is Unit 1's *"resolve issues of co-operation amongst seniors."*

> [!EXAM]
> **Skill Development** — **change management training**: **big-picture, understanding and communicating new processes, dealing with different stakeholders**
>
> **Knowledge Transfer** — **methods and means of KT across the organization**; **also track requisite knowledge of the project**
>
> **Organizational Optimization** — **promote and propose organizational changes required in line with new ERP processes** · **create roles and incentive structure** · **resolve issues**

> [!TRAP]
> **"Skills development" here is not end-user training on the software.** Read what it teaches: *"big-picture, understanding and communicating new processes, dealing with different stakeholders."*
>
> This is **training the change agents**, not the users — it is the *"conduct change management training"* activity from the Blueprinting phase. **End-user training on the ERP is a separate thing in a separate phase.** A question asking what ASAP's Skills Development process covers wants the change-management answer.

> [!INTUITION]
> **Organizational Optimization is the same content as the organizational design topic** — *"create roles and incentive structure"* is exactly *"new performance measures and incentive systems are designed."*
>
> Its definition, **"aligning jobs and processes with the new business environment"**, is a restatement of the organizational design objective: *"the architecture of the organization needs to be aligned with the new process and technology."*
>
> **So ASAP's sixth process is where the hardest and most-deferred work lives** — and giving it a named owner (the *Organizational Optimization Point* on the role chart) is precisely how a methodology stops it being deferred.

## The deck's closing note

> [!EXAM]
> On the whole set of six:
>
> > **"Make people welcome the change and ask for it."**

> [!INTUITION]
> That is the ambition of Level 2 stated as a goal, and it is a useful way to judge any change programme.
>
> **Compliance is not the target.** People can be made to use a system; that is Level 1 and it produces the *"one can take the horse to the water"* problem. **Asking for the change** means the payoff has been made real enough that they want it — which only happens once **Organizational Optimization** has actually changed the roles and incentives.
>
> Note how the six processes cover both levels: **Assessment, Sponsorship and Communications** work mostly on Level 1 fear; **Skills Development, Knowledge Transfer and Organizational Optimization** work mostly on Level 2 commitment.
