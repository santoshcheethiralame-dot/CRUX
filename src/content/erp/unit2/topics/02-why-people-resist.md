# Why People Resist Change

## The seven questions

> [!EXAM]
> **Reasons for people resisting changes** — the deck lists them as the questions employees actually ask:
>
> 1. **After the project is over, will my new skill be valued** (which I need to learn with so much effort)?
> 2. **What's in it for me? Why should I change? Why should I put so much effort to learn and implement?**
> 3. **What will be my new role? Will it be different work / more work?**
> 4. **Will I be able to operate the new system? If not, then what will happen?**
> 5. **Will my job remain?**
> 6. **Can I cope with new technologies?** *(I am an old guy — not IT savvy — understand very little of computer and IT)*
> 7. **Do I need to go back to my old place after the project? What will I do there?**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — the seven questions are really three fears.** Group them and they become recallable:
>
> | Fear | Questions | The underlying worry |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Fear for the job** | 3, 5, 7 | *Will I still have a role, and what will it be?* |
> | **Fear of the system** | 4, 6 | *Am I capable of using this thing?* |
> | **Doubt about the payoff** | 1, 2 | *Is this effort worth it for me?* |
>
> **Job, System, Payoff — J-S-P.** Every one of the seven belongs to one of the three, and each fear has a different remedy, which is exactly how the next topic is structured.

## The exam answer

> [!EXAM]
> The QnA's own model answer — **"Why do people resist change? Explain."**
>
> **People resist change for several different reasons:**
>
> **(a)** People generally ask **"What's in it for me?"** — meaning, by going through these changes, **what will I gain out of it**.
> **(b)** Many people are **afraid to adapt or learn new things, especially technology, when they are not IT savvy**.
> **(c)** A few people, although ready to learn a new skill, resist because **they think this new skill may not be useful later**.
> **(d)** Some are **afraid of their status after the ERP project ends** — whether their job will be retained, or their role.
> **(e)** Some are **anxious about using the new system**, and hence resist the change.
>
> Five points in the QnA against seven on the slide — **give the five if the question is short, and add the "new role / more work" and "go back to my old place" points for a longer answer.**

> [!TRAP]
> **"What's in it for me?" (WIIFM) is listed first in both sources**, and it is the one to lead with.
>
> It is worth noticing that this question is **entirely reasonable**. The organisation gets integration, best practices and real-time information — all of which appear in Unit 1's benefits list. **None of those twelve benefits is a benefit to the individual clerk** who now has more fields to fill in.
>
> The resistance is not irrational; it reflects a **genuine asymmetry between organisational benefit and personal cost.** That is why the answer is not persuasion but **incentives and new roles** — you have to actually put something in it for them.

> [!INTUITION]
> Reason **(c)** is the subtlest and the most often missed: *"ready to learn a new skill, but think this new skill may not be useful later."*
>
> This is a **rational investment calculation**, not fear. Learning a proprietary ERP module is effort spent on something that may be worthless if the person changes employer — unlike, say, learning a widely used technology. The employee is weighing **transferability**.
>
> Note the deck's answer to it in the strategy topic: **"having a team of power users and ERP champions"** and **"design new and more interesting roles"** — both work by making the new skill **visible and valued**, which is precisely the concern.

## Reason 7 — the returning core team member

> [!EXAM]
> *"Do I need to go back to my old place after the project? What will I do there?"*

> [!DERIVE]
> This one is specific to ERP projects and links directly to Unit 1.
>
> The **core team is drawn from the business** — Unit 1 listed as a hidden cost that a project *"typically pulls out 'good' resources from other projects/functions"*, and that **"team pull-back takes a long time."**
>
> So a core team member has spent two years away from their department. Their old job has been filled; their old skills are stale. **The project is a career risk for exactly the people you most need on it.**
>
> The deck's remedy is a **communication** item: *"inform the core team members much before the project ends what will be their new role."* Note it is *"much before the project ends"* — because if you leave it to the last month, they will spend that month job-hunting instead of finishing the handover.

## Why this matters for the project

> [!EXAM]
> Recall from Unit 1 that **"managing change" is the first of the four people challenges**, and that the **Nestlé** implementation ran aground on exactly this: *"the new business process confused most of employees, then resistance grew into rebellion in 2000."*
>
> Jeri Dunn's line is the one-sentence version of this entire topic:
>
> > *"You are challenging their principles, their beliefs and the way they have done things for many many years."*
