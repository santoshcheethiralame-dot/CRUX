# Strategies to Handle Change

## The two levels

> [!EXAM]
> **Strategy to handle change for the project team:**
>
> **First Level — Handle fear and uncertainty**
> - **Communicate**
> - **Training**
> - **Having a team of Power users and ERP champions**
> - **Round the clock help desk**
> - **Leadership support**
>
> **Second Level — Getting motivated and committed**
> - **Organizational Design**

> [!INTUITION]
> **The two levels are a ladder, and the order is the point.**
>
> **Level 1 removes the negatives** — fear, ignorance, the sense of being unsupported. **Level 2 adds the positives** — motivation, commitment, a reason to actively want the new system.
>
> You cannot skip to Level 2. **A frightened employee cannot be motivated**; incentives aimed at someone who is worried about losing their job simply do not land. Remove the fear first, then build the commitment.
>
> Map it back to the three fears from the previous topic: **Level 1 answers "fear of the system"** (training, help desk) **and part of "fear for the job"** (communication, leadership). **Level 2 answers "what's in it for me?"** — the payoff question — which is precisely why organizational design and incentives sit there.

## The QnA's model answer

> [!EXAM]
> **"What are the different strategies to handle change?"**
>
> **(a) To handle fear and uncertainty**
>   i. **For this, communication is the key**
> **(b) And training will also help majorly**
> **(c) To motivate and commit**
>   i. **For this, organizational design is the key**
>   ii. **Motivate by involving employees in discussions, incentivize, assign to new roles**
>
> Two levels, and **one "key" named for each: communication for Level 1, organizational design for Level 2.**

## The five Level-1 measures

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's own gloss on each measure, and the fear it addresses:
>
> | Measure | What it addresses |
> |---|---|
> | **Training** | **Unknown aspects, fear of ignorance** · *"how it can help me"* |
> | **Core / power users / ERP champions** | **Able to demonstrate benefits and resolve issues in a congenial way** |
> | **Help Desk (24×7)** | **People want help for trivial things to complicated issues, almost any time of the day** |
> | **Senior leadership support** | Visible backing from the top |

> [!TRAP]
> The deck makes a sharp observation about what these measures *really* achieve:
>
> > *"All these measures directly help, plus indirectly remove some of the 'insincere' efforts to avoid change."*
>
> The three excuses it names:
> - *"I was never trained"*
> - *"When this problem came, nobody helped me"*
> - *"If I don't do this new thing, no seniors see it"*
>
> Each measure **closes one excuse**: training closes the first, the 24×7 help desk closes the second, visible leadership support closes the third.
>
> **This is the honest part of the topic.** Not all resistance is sincere fear — some is convenient. Level-1 measures work on both, because they remove the *stated reason* whether or not it was the real one.

> [!INTUITION]
> **Power users and ERP champions** deserve their own note, because they solve a problem that neither training nor the help desk can.
>
> They are **peers, not trainers** — colleagues from the same department who know the work. That matters because *"able to demonstrate benefits… in a congenial way"* is credibility, not information: an objection from a peer is answered better by a peer.
>
> The deck adds the second benefit: **"Power user visibility is a good measure to involve people from the ranks and motivate."** So champions serve **both levels at once** — they reassure at Level 1 and, by being visibly valued, they answer the *"will my new skill be valued?"* resistance at Level 2.

> [!EXAM]
> **The 24×7 help desk** is also one of the **ten final checks before go-live** in Unit 1 — *"set up Help Desk / resource teams."* The two chapters are describing the same activity from two angles: **Unit 1 as a readiness gate, Unit 2 as a change-management instrument.**

## Level 2 — getting motivated and committed

> [!EXAM]
> **Getting motivated and committed to the project is a higher level of change management challenge.** The deck divides the population into three groups:
>
> 1. **A group of people who are always self-motivated**
> 2. **Another group in the core team who may be motivated for not so desirable reasons**
> 3. **A third group who want to see clearly "What's in it for me"** — i.e. why should we put so much effort into doing the project

> [!EXAM]
> **Common strategies for motivation:**
> - **Involve employees in each critical ERP decision and discussion**
> - **Design incentive schemes and performance measures** — *people need to have incentives to adopt new ways of working*
> - **Design new and more interesting roles**

> [!INTUITION]
> The three groups need **three different treatments**, which is why a single motivational message never works:
>
> - **Group 1 (self-motivated)** needs nothing but **not to be obstructed** — give them scope and get out of the way. These become your **power users**.
> - **Group 2 (motivated for the wrong reasons)** — visibility, escaping a dull job, proximity to senior management. The deck's phrasing is deliberately dry; the practical point is that their motivation is **real but fragile**, and it evaporates once the novelty does.
> - **Group 3 (WIIFM)** is the **largest and the one the strategies target.** All three listed strategies — involvement, incentives, new roles — are aimed at them.
>
> The deck's comment on the whole business is the memorable one:
>
> > **"One can take the horse to the water…"**
>
> You can mandate attendance, provide training and install the system. **You cannot mandate commitment** — which is the entire reason Level 2 exists as a separate, harder level.

> [!TRAP]
> **"People need to have incentives to adopt new ways of working"** is not a soft statement — it is a claim about **measurement systems**.
>
> If an employee's performance is still measured on the old process's numbers, then using the new system **actively hurts their appraisal**. They are behaving rationally by resisting.
>
> This is why the strategy is paired with **"performance measures"** and why the next topic — **organizational design** — is where the real leverage is. **You cannot incentivise a behaviour you still penalise.**
