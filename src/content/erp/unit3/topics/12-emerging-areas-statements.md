# Emerging Areas & Financial Statements

## The four emerging areas

> [!EXAM]
> **Emerging Areas of Financial Management:**
>
> 1. **Budgeting / Planning**
> 2. **Consolidation**
> 3. **Business Performance Management (BPM)**
> 4. **Activity Based Costing (ABC)**
>
> **These four are also called Corporate / Enterprise Performance Management [CPM / EPM]**, comprising:
> - **Performance Scorecarding — set Goals, Objectives and KPIs**
> - **Planning, Budgeting and Forecasting**
> - **Financial Consolidation and Reporting**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — the four are "B-C-B-A", and they split cleanly into forward-looking and backward-looking:**
>
> | | Direction | What it does |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Budgeting / Planning** | **forward** | what *should* happen |
> | **Business Performance Management** | **forward + back** | set goals, then measure against them |
> | **Consolidation** | **backward** | combine what *did* happen across entities |
> | **Activity Based Costing** | **backward** | attribute what *did* happen more accurately |
>
> Notice what they have in common and why they are grouped as **CPM/EPM**: none of them is *transactional*. Financial accounting records events; **these four analyse, plan and steer.** That is the shift the word *"emerging"* is pointing at — finance moving from **bookkeeping to decision support**.

> [!TRAP]
> **BPM here means Business Performance Management — not Business Process Modelling or Business Process Management**, both of which appeared in Unit 2.
>
> Three different things share the abbreviation across this course:
>
> | | Unit | Meaning |
> |---|---|---|
> | **BPM** | 2 | **Business Process Modelling** — drawing process diagrams |
> | **BPM** | 2 | **Business Process Management** — runtime process engines |
> | **BPM** | 3 | **Business Performance Management** — goals, KPIs, scorecards |
>
> Read the abbreviation **in context of the chapter**, and in a written answer expand it in full.

## Activity Based Costing

> [!EXAM]
> **ABC = Activity Based Costing**, listed under **Costing and Profitability**: *"analyse production/operating costs, **ABCs**, product profitability."*

> [!INTUITION]
> Traditional costing spreads overheads by a **single broad measure** — usually labour hours or machine hours. **ABC instead traces overheads to the specific activities that consume them**, then to the products that trigger those activities.
>
> Why it matters: a product made in **small, frequent batches** consumes far more setup, inspection and handling than a product made in long runs — but under labour-hour allocation both absorb the same overhead per hour. **Traditional costing therefore systematically over-costs the high-volume product and under-costs the low-volume one**, which can make a genuinely unprofitable product look profitable.
>
> Note the direct link back to Unit 1's *Production → Quantity, Sales → Variety* conflict: **variety carries hidden overhead cost, and ABC is what makes that cost visible** rather than smeared across everything.

## Business Performance Management and the Balanced Scorecard

> [!EXAM]
> **Business Performance Management (BPM) and Balanced Scorecard** are named together in the chapter objectives, with **Performance Scorecarding — set Goals, Objectives and KPIs** as the CPM/EPM component.

> [!INTUITION]
> The **Balanced Scorecard**'s central claim is that **financial measures alone are lagging indicators** — by the time profit falls, the causes are months old.
>
> So it *balances* the financial view with the non-financial ones that predict it: **customer, internal process, and learning & growth** perspectives alongside **financial**. Better process performance and better-trained staff show up in customer satisfaction, which eventually shows up in revenue.
>
> This is why it appears in a **finance** chapter: it takes financial reporting **out of pure accounting** and connects it to the operational data the rest of the ERP already holds. **Only an integrated system can produce a scorecard**, because three of its four perspectives are not financial data at all.

## Financial statements

> [!EXAM]
> **ERP FM supports financial statement preparation:**
>
> - **Income Statements**
> - **Profit & Loss Statement**
> - **Balance Sheet**
> - **Trial Balance**
> - **Cash Flow Statements**
> - **Shareholder Equity Statements**
>
> Plus **Accounts, Journals and Ledgers.**

> [!DERIVE]
> **The three main statements answer three different questions**, and knowing which is which is the point:
>
> | Statement | Question | Basis |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Balance Sheet** | *What do we own and owe **right now**?* | a **point in time** |
> | **Profit & Loss / Income Statement** | *Did we make money **over the period**?* | a **period** |
> | **Cash Flow Statement** | *Did **cash** actually move?* | a **period** |
>
> **The P&L and the cash flow statement differ, and that difference is the reason both exist.** A sale on credit is **revenue today** but **cash next month** — so a company can be profitable on the P&L and still run out of cash. That gap is exactly what **Treasury's liquidity management** and **FSCM's days-outstanding** work on.
>
> The **Trial Balance** is different in kind from the other three: it is not a statement for outsiders but a **check that the books balance** — total debits equal total credits — before the real statements are drawn from them.

> [!EXAM]
> Recall from the application categories that **Financial Reporting** follows **GAAP** and **IFRS**, and that statements are produced under **company laws and country specific regulations.**
>
> This is the constraint that separates financial from management accounting: **the format is imposed from outside.**

## Pulling Chapter 8 together

> [!EXAM]
> **Chapter summary:**
> - **ERP FM supports the traditional functional areas of finance** — financial accounting, management accounting, financial SCM, treasury applications
> - **FM is a highly integrated ERP module** — **other areas of ERP transactions have financial implications**
> - **Emerging areas of FM are budgeting, consolidation, ABCs, Business Performance Management**

> [!INTUITION]
> The middle bullet is the one to carry forward. **Finance is where the enterprise's activity becomes comparable.**
>
> A production order, a hire and a shipment are incommensurable as physical events — but each has a **cost**, and once expressed in money they can be added, compared and traded off. That is what makes Finance the module every other module posts into, and why **the General Ledger is the closest thing an enterprise has to a single picture of itself.**
