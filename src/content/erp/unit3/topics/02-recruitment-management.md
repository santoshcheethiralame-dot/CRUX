# Recruitment Management

## Key aspects

> [!EXAM]
> **Identify and hire the best, quickly** — and **the company needs to compete for it!**
>
> The module **needs to support different types of recruiting:**
>
> | | **Enmass (bulk)** | **Lateral hiring** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Who** | **Entry level**, recruited in bulk — **campus or otherwise** | **Special skills · experienced · senior levels** |
> | **Numbers** | Large | **Small numbers** |
> | **Timing** | **Only a few times a year** | **Through the year, based on immediate business need** |
> | **Planning** | **Long forecasting and deployment** | **Quicker** |
> | **Training** | **Extensive training** | — |
> | **Process** | Structured | **May not follow specific processes** |

> [!INTUITION]
> The two hiring types are **opposite in almost every dimension**, which is precisely why the module must support both rather than one generic flow.
>
> **Bulk hiring is a planning problem** — you commit to hundreds of offers months before the work exists, so it needs *labor forecasting* and *workforce planning*. **Lateral hiring is a speed problem** — a specific vacancy exists now and the best candidate is being courted by three other firms, so *"quicker"* is the whole requirement.
>
> Note the honest admission that lateral hiring **"may not follow specific processes."** A senior hire may be approached directly, negotiated individually, and never appear in a job portal. **The system must accommodate the exception, not just the standard path** — which is the *"multiple versions"* redesign principle from Unit 2.

## Methods of hiring

> [!EXAM]
> **All of these need to be supported by the ERP module:**
>
> - **Direct — advertisements**
> - **Campus recruitment**
> - **Employee referrals**
> - **Recruitment consultants / job portals**

## The recruitment cycle

> [!EXAM]
> **A typical cycle — the eight stages:**
>
> $$\textbf{Labor Forecasting} \rightarrow \textbf{Creating new positions} \rightarrow \textbf{Candidate Sourcing} \rightarrow \textbf{Screening / Application Tracking} \rightarrow \textbf{Interviewing} \rightarrow \textbf{Selection} \rightarrow \textbf{Job Offer} \rightarrow \textbf{Onboarding / Orientation}$$

> [!EXAM]
> **Recruitment capabilities in the HCM module of ERP applications:**
> - **Labor Forecasting**
> - **Workforce Planning**
> - **Position Management**
> - **Creating Requisitions for new positions**
> - **Candidate Sourcing**
> - **Screening and Application Tracking**
> - **Interviewing**
> - **Job Offer**
> - **Onboarding / Orientation**
> - **Recruitment analytics / MIS reports**

> [!INTUITION]
> Notice that the cycle **starts two stages before anyone applies.** *Labor forecasting* and *creating new positions* are planning activities — you must establish that a role should exist, and get it approved, before you can source for it.
>
> That is what **Position Management** and **requisitions** are for: a *position* is an authorised slot in the organisation, distinct from the *person* who may occupy it. Keeping the two separate is what lets HR answer "how many approved but unfilled roles do we have?" — and it is the same modelling distinction as an entity versus its instances.
>
> **The cycle also ends after the offer.** *Onboarding/orientation* is inside recruitment, not outside it — because an accepted offer that ends in a bad first week is a failed hire.

> [!EXAM]
> Note **"Interviewing is a part of the ERP recruitment management module"** — a stated fill-in-the-blank in the MCQ bank.

## Recruitment analytics

> [!EXAM]
> The QnA asks for **common examples of recruitment analytics**:
> 1. **Cost per hire**
> 2. **Time to fill vacant positions**
> 3. **Onboarding efficiency**

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's fuller note on what recruitment reporting and analytics cover:
>
> **Reports:** *"data related to how many needed, by when, how many done; how long it takes to recruit, has the internal SLA/OLA been met?"*
>
> **Analytics:** *"identifying **which sources have higher yield**, which process is more efficient, what is the probability of meeting the hiring target, **which process gives better candidates in the long run**. For this, it has to integrate well with the performance and employee career tracking process."*

> [!DERIVE]
> **The last sentence is the one worth understanding, because it is an integration argument.**
>
> *"Which process gives better candidates in the long run"* cannot be answered by the recruitment module alone. You need to know how those hires **performed years later** — data that lives in **performance management** and **career tracking**.
>
> So the question requires joining **recruitment data to performance data over a multi-year window** — precisely the kind of query that is impossible across information silos and trivial on a **common database**. It is Unit 1's *driver C* producing a concrete capability.
>
> **Cost per hire and time to fill are operational metrics; "which source yields the best long-run employees" is a strategic one** — and only integration makes it askable.

## Automation benefits

> [!EXAM]
> **Recruitment module — automation benefits:**
> - **Completely electronic recruitment process eliminating all paper documents**
> - **Using the internet to attract candidates** (company website, job portal, etc.)
> - **Automating all record-keeping** (digital records of candidate's profile, certificates, etc.)
> - **Better collaboration between recruiters and hiring managers**, so that managers can **optimize their time by focusing on best candidates only**
> - **Quicker transfer of information to employee database**
> - **Stricter & automated verification on certain aspects**
> - **Better maintenance of accuracy and "currency" of data**

> [!INTUITION]
> Two of these repay a second look.
>
> **"Quicker transfer of information to employee database"** is the integration benefit. In a paper process, everything collected during recruitment — qualifications, certificates, personal details — is **re-keyed** when the person joins. With an integrated module the candidate record **becomes** the employee record. That is *"enter once, used everywhere"* at the moment of hire.
>
> **"Better collaboration… so that managers can focus on best candidates only"** connects straight back to the overview: the HR function is now **distributed to line managers**, who are doing recruitment on top of their real jobs. Their time is the scarce resource, so the system's job is to **filter before it reaches them.**

> [!TRAP]
> **"Currency" of data** — in quotation marks in the deck — means **how up to date it is**, not money. Candidate data goes stale quickly: people change jobs, phone numbers and salary expectations. A recruitment database that is not maintained becomes a liability rather than an asset.
