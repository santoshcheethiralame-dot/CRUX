# Personnel Management & Employee Self-Service

## Employee personnel management

> [!EXAM]
> **Employee Personnel Management covers:**
> - **Labour relations** — **grievances and discipline**
> - **Non-employee administration** — **tracking and managing non-payroll workers**
> - **Maintaining detailed employee records**
>
> From the HCM wheel's notes, it also handles: **handling of resignation and retirement — termination, superannuation**, and **policy administration, disciplinary actions**.

> [!INTUITION]
> **Personnel management is the "record of truth" area** — the master data of HCM. Everything else in the wheel reads from it: payroll needs the grade, scheduling needs the skills, performance needs the reporting line.
>
> That is why *"managing employee record"* sits in the **very first wave of automation**, alongside payroll. You cannot automate anything about a person until the person exists in the system.

> [!TRAP]
> **"Non-employee administration — tracking and managing non-payroll workers"** is the item most often overlooked, and it is increasingly the largest gap in practice.
>
> Contractors, consultants, agency staff and vendor personnel **work in your building, use your systems and create your safety obligations — but are not on your payroll.** A system keyed entirely on "employee" cannot see them, which means they are invisible to headcount reporting, access control and **EHS incident tracking**.
>
> Note how this connects to Unit 1's implementation costs: consultants were the single largest line item, so a large ERP project is itself full of non-payroll workers.

> [!EXAM]
> **"Employee self service is a part of personnel management"** — **TRUE**, a stated MCQ-bank item and one of the pasted questions. ESS is treated as part of the personnel management area even though it is drawn separately on the HCM wheel.

## Employee Relationship Management

> [!EXAM]
> **Employee Relationship Management / Employee Self-Service (ESS):**
> - **Enhance the relationships of the employee to the enterprise, managers, co-workers and external parties**
> - **A common way of achieving this is by deploying employee portals that connect the employee to relevant information, resources and people**
> - **Achieve higher levels of employee satisfaction by delivering online services and making employees more productive**

> [!INTUITION]
> The name **"Employee Relationship Management"** is deliberately parallel to **Customer Relationship Management** — and the parallel is the insight.
>
> CRM exists because the customer relationship is valuable and worth managing systematically. **ERM applies the same reasoning to employees**: the relationship has value, it can be measured, and it can be improved by giving people **self-directed access** rather than making them queue at an HR desk.
>
> Note the four relationships it targets — **to the enterprise, to managers, to co-workers, and to external parties.** It is not only employee-to-HR.

## The employee portal

> [!EXAM]
> **Employee Portal:** **a personalizable portal designed for employee communications, content management, and integration to self-service applications.**
>
> **Gartner's definition of a portal** *(from the deck's notes)*:
>
> > **"Access to and interaction with relevant information, applications and business processes, by select targeted audiences, in a highly personalized manner."**

> [!DERIVE]
> **Gartner's definition has four load-bearing parts**, and each is a design requirement:
>
> | Phrase | What it demands |
> |---|---|
> | **Access to *and interaction with*** | not just reading — you must be able to **transact** |
> | **information, applications and business processes** | it aggregates **three different kinds of thing**, not just documents |
> | **select targeted audiences** | different groups see different things — **role-based** |
> | **in a highly personalized manner** | and within a group, **each individual's view differs** |
>
> The distinction between the last two is the subtle one. **Targeting** is by *role* — managers see approval queues, everyone else does not. **Personalisation** is by *individual* — your leave balance, your payslip, your training plan.
>
> **A portal that only shows information is a noticeboard; the "interaction" is what makes it self-service.**

## What ESS actually does

> [!EXAM]
> **Employee Self-Service (ESS):**
> - **Benefits**
> - **Maintenance of personal data**
> - **Leave application**
> - **Accessing pay slip and payroll data**
> - **Registering to a new training**
>
> **Employees maintain all these data and do all these transactions themselves.**
>
> **Also used to facilitate many compliance needs** such as **policy awareness, information security**.

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Employees maintain all these data themselves"** is the whole economic argument, and it is a **data quality** argument before it is a cost one.
>
> The person who knows your new address is **you**. Routing that change through an HR clerk adds a delay, a transcription error, and a cost — and produces data that is no fresher than the day you got round to telling them. **Self-service moves data entry to the person with the knowledge.**
>
> Trace the ESS list against the wheel and you will see it **cuts across every area**: benefits (Benefits & Payroll), personal data (Personnel Mgmt), leave (Time & Leave), payslip (Payroll), training registration (Learning Mgmt). **ESS is not an area so much as a channel into all of them** — which is why it is drawn on the rim of the wheel.

> [!TRAP]
> **The compliance use is the one students miss** — *"policy awareness, information security."*
>
> Many obligations require an organisation to prove that **every employee was informed** of a policy and acknowledged it. Doing that on paper for ten thousand people is impractical; doing it through a portal produces a **timestamped, auditable record** as a by-product.
>
> This is the chapter summary's third bullet — *"supports maintenance of records for the compliance aspects"* — in its most concrete form.
