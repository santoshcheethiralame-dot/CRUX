# Talent, Performance & Learning Management

## Talent management

> [!EXAM]
> **Talent management provides an integrated set of applications for:**
>
> **strategy · assessment · competency management · performance · career planning · succession planning · learning and development**
>
> **Talent Management covers:**
> - **Performance Management**
> - **Competency Assessment**
> - **Career Planning**
> - **Succession Planning**
> - **Skill Training** (technical and non-technical)
> - **Fast-tracking of high performers to identify key contributors and future leaders**

> [!TRAP]
> **Succession planning is part of Talent Management, NOT Learning Management.** The MCQ bank states it as a True/False:
>
> > *"Succession planning is a part of learning management."* — **FALSE.**
>
> The two are adjacent and easy to confuse. **Learning management is about delivering training**; **succession planning is about identifying who replaces whom** — a talent decision, not a training activity. Note that *skill training* appears under Talent Management too, which is what makes the boundary slippery.

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Fast-tracking of high performers to identify key contributors and future leaders"** is the clearest statement of what talent management is *for*.
>
> Ordinary HR administration treats employees uniformly — same leave rules, same payroll run. **Talent management deliberately treats them differently**, identifying a subset for accelerated development.
>
> That is why it sits in the **third and last wave of automation**: the judgement of who is a future leader cannot be computed, only **recorded, routed and tracked**. The system supports the decision; it does not make it.

## Performance management

> [!EXAM]
> **Performance Management covers:**
> - **Performance measurements — direct / indirect inputs, 360 degree appraisals**
> - **Establishment of roles & responsibilities**
> - **Objective parameters**
> - **Goal setting**

> [!EXAM]
> **"360 degree performance evaluation is a part of the ERP performance management module."** — **TRUE**, a stated MCQ-bank item and one of the pasted questions.

> [!INTUITION]
> **360-degree appraisal means feedback from all directions** — manager, peers, subordinates and sometimes customers — rather than only from the person above you.
>
> It exists because of the **distributed HR function** from the overview: in knowledge work, your manager may see only a fraction of what you do, while your peers see the rest. **The measurement problem is a visibility problem**, and 360 solves it by widening the set of observers.
>
> It also makes the *system* argument: collecting, anonymising and aggregating feedback from a dozen people per employee, across thousands of employees, is administratively impossible on paper and routine in software.

> [!EXAM]
> **ERP Talent / Performance Management module areas:**
> - **Career Development Planning**
> - **Succession Planning**
> - **Competency Management**
> - **Performance Appraisal / Performance Management**
> - **Goal Alignment**
> - **Performance Management Analytics**

> [!DERIVE]
> The deck's note explains why **two levels** of support are needed:
>
> > *"Performance management should work at **both individual level and company level** — so support is required for both. For example, an **individual** is interested to get rated on a project; **HR** wants to track how many people suffer from communication problems."*
>
> **Same data, two entirely different questions.** The individual asks *"how did I do?"*; the organisation asks *"where are we systematically weak?"*
>
> The second question is only answerable by **aggregating across all appraisals** — and it produces an *action* the individual view never could: if 200 people are rated weak on communication, that is a **training programme**, not 200 conversations. This is the bridge from **Performance Management to Learning Management**, and it is why they sit together in the same wave.

> [!EXAM]
> **Goal Alignment** is the item worth naming explicitly: it is the mechanism that connects an individual's objectives upward to the department's and the enterprise's — the antidote to the *Production → Quantity, Sales → Variety* conflict of Unit 1, applied at the level of a single person's targets.

## Learning management

> [!EXAM]
> **ERP HR Learning Management areas — the eight:**
>
> 1. **Online Course Catalog**
> 2. **Registration to Training**
> 3. **Content Management**
> 4. **Training Collaboration**
> 5. **Training Assessment**
> 6. **Tracking Training Progress**
> 7. **Supporting Training Financials**
> 8. **Reports**

> [!INTUITION]
> **Mnemonic — walk one employee through one course**, and the eight areas fall out in order:
>
> **Browse** the catalog → **register** → consume the **content** → **collaborate** with others → be **assessed** → have **progress tracked** → someone pays the **financials** → management reads the **reports**.
>
> Note that **only two of the eight are about learning content itself.** The rest are catalogue, enrolment, tracking, cost and reporting — i.e. **administration**. That is the honest shape of an LMS inside an ERP: its value is in *managing* training at scale, not in teaching.

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's note on the market:
>
> > *"In the Learning area there are many **proprietary LMS — Learning Management System — software**. Generally they are quite versatile."*
>
> This is the same pattern as HCM generally: **specialist vendors thrive where the ERP module is a late or partial entrant.**

> [!EXAM]
> From the wider automation list, learning also includes **Training Administration · Managing/Developing Training Content · Online Learning · Individual Employee Learning Plan · Knowledge Management / Building a Learning Organization.**

> [!TRAP]
> **"Knowledge management / building a learning organization"** is the most strategic item in the whole HCM chapter and the least automatable.
>
> A system can deliver a course and record a score. It cannot make an organisation *learn* — that requires the knowledge-transfer and culture work that Unit 2's change management chapter dealt with. **Which is exactly why learning management is automated last.**
