# CRM — Concept & Benefits

## What CRM is

> [!EXAM]
> - **Customer Relationship Management (CRM) is a strategy for managing a company's interactions with its customers and sales prospects**
> - **Often CRM is equated with automating business processes related to sales, marketing and customer service — CRM is not just about technology**
> - **It involves strategy and redesign of business processes**, with the overall goal to **find, attract, and win new customers, retaining those customers, and finally getting feedback from them to better design the company's product and customer facing processes**

> [!INTUITION]
> **The goal statement is a four-stage life cycle, and reading it that way makes the whole chapter fall into place:**
>
> $$\textbf{find} \rightarrow \textbf{attract and win} \rightarrow \textbf{retain} \rightarrow \textbf{learn from}$$
>
> | Stage | Which CRM application serves it |
> |---|---|
> | **find** | **Marketing Management** — campaigns, lead generation |
> | **attract and win** | **Sales Force Automation** — leads, opportunities, quotes |
> | **retain** | **Call Centre · Field Service** — after-sales support |
> | **learn from** | **CRM Analytics** — sales, marketing and service analytics |
>
> **The application areas are not an arbitrary list — they are the customer life cycle with software attached**, exactly as HCM's ten areas were the employee life cycle (**Hire-to-Retire**) with software attached.
>
> **And the last stage is the one people forget.** *"Getting feedback from them to better design the company's product and customer facing processes"* means CRM is not only about selling more — **it is an input to product design.** That is what makes it a *strategy* rather than a sales tool.

> [!TRAP]
> ***"CRM is not just about technology"*** is the single most examinable sentence in this chapter, and the deck says it twice — once directly, and once as *"it involves **strategy and redesign of business processes**."*
>
> **This is exactly the argument Unit 2 made about ERP itself:** buying the software achieves nothing unless the underlying processes change. A company that installs a CRM but keeps its sales people hoarding contacts in personal notebooks has bought a database nobody fills in.
>
> **The parallel is worth stating in an answer:** *ERP without BPR fails; CRM without process redesign fails; and for the same reason — **the value is in the changed behaviour, not the installed software.***

## People, process and technology

> [!EXAM]
> - **CRM uses people, processes, and technology to gain insight into the behavior of customers**
> - **CRM can bring together information from all data sources within an organization (and from outside the organization if needed, like social media) to give one holistic view of the customer in real time**
> - **CRM allows customer facing employees from sales, customer support, and marketing to make informed decisions on everything from cross selling and upselling opportunities, to target marketing strategies, to competitive positioning tactics**
> - **CRM can also reduce the costs of marketing, sales and customer service**

> [!DERIVE]
> **"One holistic view of the customer" is CRM's central claim, and it is the customer-side version of ERP's founding argument.**
>
> Without it, the same customer exists as **several different customers** in several different systems:
>
> | Department | What they know |
> |---|---|
> | **Sales** | the deals, the pipeline, the discount history |
> | **Support** | the complaints, the open tickets |
> | **Marketing** | which campaigns they responded to |
> | **Finance** | what they owe and how late they pay |
>
> **The failure mode is concrete and familiar:** a salesperson calls to upsell a customer who has an unresolved support complaint and an overdue invoice — **because the salesperson cannot see either.**
>
> $$\textbf{fragmented customer data} \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{the company contradicts itself in front of the customer}$$
>
> **This is precisely Unit 1's information-silo problem, moved from the factory to the customer.** Nestlé's *"29 different vanilla records"* is the same defect: **one real-world entity, many system identities.** ERP unified the *internal* view; **CRM unifies the customer view** — and the deck adds *"from outside the organization if needed, like social media"*, which ERP never claimed.
>
> **Note "in real time."** A nightly-batch view of the customer is not enough when the customer is on the phone right now — which is why the call-centre application's most valuable feature is **"previous records of the caller"**, available while the call is live.

> [!EXAM]
> **The last bullet is easy to miss and worth remembering: *"CRM can also reduce the costs of marketing, sales and customer service."***
>
> **The business case is not only revenue.** Better targeting means **less wasted marketing spend**; self-service means **fewer calls to answer**; one customer view means **less time spent hunting for information.** Both sides of the ledger.

## The eight benefits

> [!EXAM]
> **CRM Benefits:**
>
> 1. **Better profitability / revenue per customer**
> 2. **Improved Customer Service / Satisfaction**
> 3. **Higher Productivity**
> 4. **Focused Marketing**
> 5. **Better Cross Sell / Up Sell**
> 6. **Better Opportunity Close**
> 7. **Reduced Sales and Marketing Expenses**
> 8. **Increased Customer Retention / Loyalty**

> [!INTUITION]
> **The eight benefits are four pairs — a revenue benefit and a cost benefit at each stage of the funnel:**
>
> | Stage | **Earn more** | **Spend less** |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Reaching people** | **Focused Marketing** | **Reduced Sales and Marketing Expenses** |
> | **Winning them** | **Better Opportunity Close** | **Higher Productivity** |
> | **Growing them** | **Better Cross Sell / Up Sell** · **Better profitability per customer** | — |
> | **Keeping them** | **Increased Retention / Loyalty** | **Improved Customer Service** |
>
> **Focused marketing and reduced marketing expense are the same mechanism seen twice:** if you know which customers are likely to buy, you **stop spending on the ones who are not**. Revenue up and cost down from one improvement.
>
> **"Better profitability / revenue per customer" is listed first and is the most strategic**, because it reframes what a customer is. Treating a customer as **a stream of revenue over years** rather than **a transaction** is what justifies spending on retention at all — and it is why *retention* and *loyalty* appear as benefits in their own right.

> [!TRAP]
> **Distinguish cross-sell from up-sell** — the deck pairs them and they are different:
>
> - **Cross-sell** — sell them a **different** product. *You bought a laptop; here is a printer.*
> - **Up-sell** — sell them a **better version** of the same thing. *You are looking at the 8GB model; consider the 16GB.*
>
> **Both are cheap revenue** compared with acquiring a new customer, which is why they get a benefit line of their own — and both **require the single customer view** to work at all. You cannot cross-sell without knowing what they already own.

## The sales funnel

> [!EXAM]
> **Topic terminology — the sales funnel and lead conversion:**
>
> $$\textbf{Leads} \rightarrow \textbf{Prospects} \rightarrow \textbf{Opportunities} \;[\textit{deals}] \rightarrow \textbf{Customer} \;[\textit{Sales Order}] \rightarrow \textbf{Account} \;[\textit{and Cases}] \rightarrow \textbf{Repeat Sales / Customer}$$
>
> **And the deck's vocabulary list:** *"Close the deal, in less time"* — **cold calls · contact · customer satisfaction · engagement · follow up/through · improve business, boost profits · leads (catch 'em all, hot leads) · loyal customers · opportunity · pipelines · prospects · relationships · retention, acquisition · sales funnel · strategy**

> [!DERIVE]
> **The funnel is a sequence of increasing commitment, and each stage has a precise meaning worth knowing.**
>
> | Stage | Means |
> |---|---|
> | **Lead** | a **name** — someone who might conceivably buy. Unqualified. |
> | **Prospect** | a lead that has been **qualified** — they have the need, the budget and the authority |
> | **Opportunity** *(deal)* | a **specific potential sale**, with a value and an expected close date |
> | **Customer** | the opportunity **closed** — a **Sales Order** exists |
> | **Account** *(and Cases)* | an **ongoing relationship** — support tickets are *cases* against the account |
> | **Repeat Sales** | the relationship **generates more revenue** without re-acquisition |
>
> **Why it is drawn as a funnel: the volume shrinks at every stage.** Thousands of leads become hundreds of prospects, dozens of opportunities, and a handful of customers.
>
> **Two practical consequences follow, and they explain two whole application areas:**
>
> - **Conversion rate between stages is the measurable thing** — which is what *pipeline* and *sales forecasting* in SFA actually compute. If 10% of opportunities close and you have ₹10 crore in the pipeline, the forecast is ₹1 crore.
> - **The transition from Customer to Account is where CRM stops being about selling.** After the sale, the same record accumulates **cases** — support interactions — and the goal changes from *win* to *retain*. **That is the boundary between the sales applications and the service applications**, and it is why call centre and field service exist alongside SFA.
