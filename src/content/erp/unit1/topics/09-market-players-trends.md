# Market Players, Industries & Emerging Trends

## The vendor landscape

> [!NOTE]
> **Product companies develop and deliver ERP software**, and the deck separates them into **Leaders** and **Major Players**.
>
> The **2024 ERP market map** shows, among others: **SAP · Oracle · Microsoft · Infor · IFS · Epicor · Sage Intacct · Acumatica · Odoo · Unit4 · QAD · Plex · Aptean · ECI · Priority · RealPage · Rockwell Automation · ServiceNow · Pega · Sabre · BMC · Access**

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's own summary of the market structure, from the trends slide:
>
> > **"Stratification of products — SAP, Oracle & the rest."**
>
> **SAP and Oracle are the two Tier-1 leaders; everyone else competes below them.** If asked to name major ERP players, lead with **SAP and Oracle**, then add **Microsoft (Dynamics)** and **Infor**.

> [!INTUITION]
> The two names to attach to specifics:
>
> - **SAP** owns the **ASAP** implementation methodology, and its abbreviation appears in the MCQ bank as a fill-in-the-blank. *"ASAP roadmap is a detailed project plan by **SAP**"* — an MCQ answer where the distractors are Oracle, PeopleSoft and Baan.
> - **Oracle** owns **AIM / Fast Forward**, and is the vendor in the **Agilent** case study (Oracle E-Business Suite).
>
> **Nestlé used SAP; Agilent used Oracle.** Two case studies, two vendors — a neat way to keep them straight.

> The deck also shows **Gartner's Magic Quadrant for Cloud ERP for Service-Centric Enterprises**, a **comparison of ERP companies by Gartner**, and a **comparison of top ERP companies by ElevatIQ (2024)**. The examinable takeaway is not the individual positions but that **independent analyst rankings exist and are used in package selection** — which is Unit 3's subject.

> [!TRAP]
> This slide exists to kill the misconception that **"ERP is only for manufacturing companies."**
>
> Six manufacturing verticals and **six services verticals**, deliberately balanced. The **banking home-loan example** — one event updating ten modules — is the worked proof for the services side, and **BFSI** is now one of the largest ERP markets there is.
>
> If an exam asks *"is ERP only for manufacturing?"*, the structure of a full answer is: **no** → the belief comes from the **MRP → MRP II → ERP** origin → and here are six service industries that use it.

## Emerging trends in the ERP marketplace

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's ten trends:
>
> 1. **Stratification of products** — SAP, Oracle & the rest
> 2. **Mainline ERPs adding functions all the time**
> 3. **Niche players under pressure**
> 4. **SME focus**
> 5. **Verticalized features**
> 6. **New deployment models — Cloud ERP**
> 7. **Maintenance itself as a (sizeable) service**
> 8. **Newer types of ERP projects emerging** — **Migration, Consolidation and Harmonization, Upgrades**
> 9. **Embedded AI**
> 10. **Data, Analytics and Process Intelligence**

> [!INTUITION]
> The ten trends group into **four stories**, which is far easier to reproduce than a list:
>
> | Story | Trends |
> |---|---|
> | **The market is consolidating** | stratification (1) · mainline ERPs adding functions (2) · niche players under pressure (3) |
> | **The market is broadening downward** | SME focus (4) · verticalized features (5) |
> | **The delivery model is changing** | cloud ERP (6) · maintenance as a service (7) |
> | **The work itself is changing** | new project types (8) · embedded AI (9) · analytics & process intelligence (10) |
>
> Note **1–3 and 4–5 pull in opposite directions**, and both are true at once: the *top* of the market concentrates around SAP and Oracle while the *bottom* opens up to smaller companies. **The middle — the niche players — is what gets squeezed.**

> [!DERIVE]
> **Trend 8 is the one with a direct consequence elsewhere in the unit.**
>
> *"Newer types of ERP projects are emerging: Migration, Consolidation and Harmonization, Upgrades."* This is a market that has **matured** — most large enterprises already run an ERP, so the growth is no longer in **first-time implementations** but in **changing the one you have**.
>
> That is exactly the **types of ERP projects** list in Chapter 2, and it is why the MCQ bank asks about *"ERP consolidation projects"* where **companies migrate from several ERPs to one**.
>
> **A first-time implementation and a consolidation are different projects and need different methodologies** — which is the question bank's *"why do different ERP projects need different types of methodologies?"*

> [!EXAM]
> Trends **9 and 10 — embedded AI, and data/analytics/process intelligence** — are the newest additions and map directly onto **course objective 4**: *"to be aware of current trends like Role of Analytics, AI, Cloud and other technologies employed in EBS."* Expect at least one question on them.

## Topic summary — Chapter 1

> [!EXAM]
> The deck's own closing summary, worth reproducing as a final-answer paragraph:
>
> - **ERP is a strategic application of IT in modern enterprises**
> - **Evolved by business environment growth along with IT progress**
> - **Key benefit is integration and business standardization**
> - **ERP is a very large, integrated, sophisticated, "business aware" application system**
> - **ERP is a combination of technology and business process models in equal measure**
> - **Is a good example of a socio-technical system**

> [!INTUITION]
> **"Socio-technical system"** is the phrase to carry into Chapter 2, and it is the thesis of the entire unit.
>
> A socio-technical system is one where **the social half and the technical half must be designed together** — neither works alone. It explains why the challenges are grouped into *implementation, support and **people***; why *change management* gets its own unit; and why **both case studies reach the same conclusion**:
>
> > Nestlé: *"No major software implementation is really about the software."*
> > Agilent: *"ERP implementations are a lot more than software packages. People, processes, policies and culture are all factors."*
>
> **Two different companies, two different vendors, two different decades, one lesson.**
