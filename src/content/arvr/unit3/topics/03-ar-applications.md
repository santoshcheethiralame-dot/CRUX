# Key Applications of AR

> AR has found **diverse applications across multiple domains**, from industrial manufacturing to retail, healthcare and entertainment.

Each of the five comes with a named example — **learn the example, not just the domain**, because that is what turns a generic answer into a specific one.

## 1. Industry & Construction

> AR is widely used in industrial settings to provide **real-time overlays of assembly instructions, maintenance procedures and schematic diagrams** directly onto equipment or machinery.
>
> **Example:** **Boeing** used AR systems to guide technicians during the complex task of **wire bundle assembly in aircraft**, **significantly reducing errors and improving efficiency**.

> [!NOTE]
> This is the same Boeing work that **gave the field its name in 1992** — the origin story and the flagship industrial application are one and the same.

## 2. Medical

> In healthcare, AR enables doctors and surgeons to **visualize internal anatomical structures without invasive procedures**. It assists in **preoperative planning, surgical navigation and medical training**.
>
> **Example:** using AR to display **fetal imaging data on a pregnant patient's abdomen**, allowing clinicians to monitor fetal health and development **non-invasively**.

> [!INTUITION]
> The fetal-imaging case (UNC, 1994) is the clearest illustration of **why registration matters**. Displaying an ultrasound on a nearby monitor is not AR — it is a screen. Displaying it **anchored to the patient's body, in the correct place, from the clinician's viewpoint** is AR, and the entire clinical value comes from that alignment being *accurate*. A few centimetres of registration error would make it worse than useless.

## 3. Navigation

> AR enhances navigation by **overlaying digital route guidance directly onto the real-world environment**. Early systems like the **Touring Machine (1997)** pioneered this, displaying real-time directions within a head-mounted display as users moved through outdoor environments. Modern AR navigation apps on smartphones and smart glasses provide **dynamic, context-aware directions for pedestrians, drivers and cyclists**.

## 4. Gaming

> One of the most popular uses of AR, creating **immersive and interactive experiences that merge virtual game elements with the real world**.
>
> **Early examples:** **ARQuake** — a modified version of Quake projected into physical environments — and **Invisible Trains**, a handheld AR game for PDAs.
> **Contemporary:** mobile games like **Pokémon GO**, which **popularized AR gaming for the masses**.

## 5. Retail

> AR has transformed retail by letting customers **virtually try on clothes, accessories or makeup**, and preview how **furniture or home décor** will look in their spaces.
>
> **Example:** **IKEA Place** enables users to place virtual furniture in their homes through their smartphones to assess **size, colour and style before purchasing** — improving customer engagement and **reducing product returns**.

> [!INTUITION]
> Retail AR is the clearest **commercial** justification of the technology: a returned sofa costs the retailer shipping both ways plus restocking. If AR prevents even a small fraction of returns it pays for itself. Notice that the value comes from **correct scale** — which again is a registration and tracking problem, not a graphics one.

> [!EXAM]
> *"Explain the key applications of augmented reality"* — for each domain give **what AR contributes** and **the named example**. Five domains × two facts is a comfortable 5–8 marks. If you have room, close by noting that the successful applications all share one property: **the value depends on virtual content being correctly registered to a real object** — the surgeon's anatomy, the technician's cable, the customer's living room.
