# Applications of Virtual Reality

> VR has made significant inroads across **multiple sectors beyond entertainment**, offering unique solutions for **education, healthcare and professional training**.

## 1. Gaming

> VR gaming provides **highly immersive and interactive entertainment** by placing players **directly inside virtual worlds**. These games often employ **motion controllers, haptic feedback and room-scale tracking** for enhanced realism.
>
> **Example: Beat Saber** — a rhythm-based VR game where players slice through flying blocks in time with music, offering a **fast-paced, physically engaging** experience.

> [!NOTE]
> Beat Saber is a good example for a different reason than it seems: it is deliberately **low-realism**. Abstract blocks in an abstract space — no uncanny valley, no artificial locomotion (you stand still and swing), and therefore **almost no motion sickness**. It is a masterclass in designing *around* VR's weaknesses rather than fighting them.

## 2. Education

> VR enhances education by offering students **interactive, immersive learning experiences impossible in traditional settings**. Users can **explore historical landmarks, perform virtual experiments, or simulate dangerous scenarios safely**.
>
> **Example:** virtual tours like the **Nimrud Palace** VR experience transport users to **ancient Mesopotamian ruins**, allowing them to explore reconstructed archaeological sites with **historical annotations**.

> [!INTUITION]
> The educational argument rests on three things VR uniquely offers: **scale** (stand inside a cell, or a cathedral), **danger** (rehearse a chemical spill safely), and **impossibility** (visit a site that no longer exists). Nimrud is the third case — much of the real site was destroyed, so the reconstruction is the only way to visit it at all.

## 3. Healthcare

> VR is used for **surgical simulation, patient therapy and medical education**. It enables practitioners to **practise complex procedures in risk-free virtual environments** and provides therapeutic applications for mental health treatment, such as **exposure therapy for PTSD or phobia management**.
>
> **Examples:** VR-based **surgical training systems** let medical students rehearse procedures in simulated operating rooms. Applications like **Clouds Over Sidra** use VR for **empathy-building** in humanitarian work, placing users in the life of a **12-year-old Syrian refugee**.

> [!INTUITION]
> Healthcare uses VR in two quite different ways, and it is worth separating them:
> - **Skill acquisition** — surgical simulation. The value is **repetition without risk**; a trainee can fail a procedure a hundred times.
> - **Psychological intervention** — exposure therapy and empathy. The value is **presence itself**: a phobia responds to a virtual spider precisely because presence makes the fear response genuine, and *Clouds Over Sidra* works because being *there* is emotionally different from watching a documentary.
>
> The second category is the strongest practical argument that presence is real and measurable.

## 4. Telepresence and remote collaboration

> VR facilitates **remote interaction and collaboration** by enabling users to **share a common virtual space regardless of physical location**. Particularly valuable for **meetings, product design and virtual tourism**.
>
> **Example:** VR-enabled **telepresence robots** with **panoramic video feeds and spatial audio** allow remote participants to attend meetings, conferences or site inspections **as if physically present**.

> [!NOTE]
> Note the mention of **spatial audio** — sound placed in 3D so voices come from where the speaker is. It is what makes a multi-person virtual meeting followable rather than a wall of noise, and it is covered in Unit 4.

## The summary table

| Domain | What VR contributes | Named example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gaming** | Immersive interactive entertainment; motion controllers, haptics, room-scale tracking | **Beat Saber** |
| **Education** | Explore landmarks, run experiments, simulate danger safely | **Nimrud Palace** virtual tour |
| **Healthcare** | Surgical simulation, therapy (PTSD, phobias), medical education | **Surgical training**; **Clouds Over Sidra** |
| **Telepresence** | Shared virtual space regardless of location; meetings, design review, tourism | **Telepresence robots** with panoramic video and spatial audio |

## AR applications vs VR applications

Worth noticing, because it explains the split:

| | **AR is chosen when** | **VR is chosen when** |
|---|---|---|
| The task | is **anchored to a real object** — this cable, this patient, this room | involves a place or situation that is **absent, dangerous or impossible** |
| The user | must **stay in the real world** to do their job | can safely be **removed from it** |
| Examples | Boeing wiring, surgical navigation, IKEA Place | Nimrud, surgical *simulation*, exposure therapy |

> [!EXAM]
> *"Explain the applications of virtual reality"* — four domains, each with **contribution + named example**. If the question asks you to compare AR and VR applications, use the table above: **AR augments a task you are already doing in the real world; VR substitutes a world you cannot otherwise be in.**
