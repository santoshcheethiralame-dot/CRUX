# Software Patents

## What is a patent?

> [!NOTE]
> A **patent** is an exclusive right granted for an invention — a product or process — that is **New, Useful, and Non-obvious**. To get one, technical information about the invention must be **disclosed to the public** in a patent application.

**What rights does it grant?** A patent does **not** give you the right to make/use/sell your invention. It gives you the right to **exclude others** from making, using, or selling it for the **term (20 years)** — letting you sue, license, or collect royalties.

> [!EXAM]
> A patent must be **New, Useful, Non-obvious** and lasts **20 years**. Crucially, it grants the **right to exclude others**, not the right to practice the invention yourself.

## Patents vs Copyright

| | **Copyright** | **Patent** |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | The *expression* of a work | The *idea* and its use |
| Ideas? | Does **not** cover ideas | **Only** covers ideas |
| How obtained | **Automatic** | **Issued on application** by a patent office |

## Why patents exist

Encourage **disclosure** of inventions · reward invention/creativity · protect **R&D investment** · encourage the market to **"design around"** · protect small companies from large ones.

## "The software patent system is broken"

The landmark case is **Alice v. CLS Bank (2014)**. The problems:

| Problem | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Weak non-obviousness** | Trivial "do X on a computer" patents (running bingo, ordering pizza with toppings) |
| **Long pendencies & terms** | 20-year terms in a field that changes in months |
| **Incompatibility** | PNG invented to dodge **GIF** patents; Opus vs MP3; AV1 vs H.265 |
| **Independent discovery doesn't matter** | You can infringe by solving a problem yourself (John Carmack: *"horrifying"*) |
| **Only large orgs benefit** | US software patents cost **$15k–$45k**; challenging bad patents needs time/money/lawyers |
| **Patent trolls (NPEs)** | **Non-Practicing Entities** sue over vague patents — WiFi in cafés, autocomplete, "scan-and-email" |
| **Innovation stifled** | Vague, non-technical legal language obfuscates the patent and lets it be stretched |

> [!TRAP]
> **Patent trolls (Non-Practicing Entities)** don't build products — they buy vague patents and sue. Because the US system makes **each party pay its own legal fees**, it's often cheaper to **settle** than fight, even against a bogus patent. This is a tax on real innovation.

> [!INTUITION]
> Responses to the broken system include defensive arrangements — the **Defensive Patent License (DPL)**, the **Open Invention Network (OIN)**, **License on Transfer (LOT)** — plus calls to reform or even **abolish software patents** entirely. *(Open source is "under attack too" from patent suits.)*
