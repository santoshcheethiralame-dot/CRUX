# Codes of Ethics & Human Flourishing

## Open intellectual-property concerns (LLMs)

The rise of large language models raises fresh ethical questions:
- Was the **data** used to train these LLMs obtained **legally**?
- Who owns the **IP** of LLM **outputs**?
- Should **sensitive information** be provided as **inputs** to LLMs?

## The ACM Code of Ethics

> [!NOTE]
> **As an ACM member I will…**
> - **Contribute to society and human well-being**
> - **Avoid harm** to others
> - Be **honest and trustworthy**
> - Be **fair** and take action **not to discriminate**
> - **Honor property rights** (copyrights, patents)
> - Give **proper credit** for intellectual property
> - **Respect the privacy** of others
> - **Honor confidentiality**

> [!TRAP]
> A sobering research finding: **a code of ethics does not appear to change the decisions** software developers actually make. Knowing the principles isn't enough — ethics has to be **practised and reinforced**, not just published on a wall.

## The challenge & human flourishing

> [!INTUITION]
> **How do you apply ethics to a field that changes so fast?** The **ADA** disability-access law was written in 1990 — *before the first website* — yet was applied to **Domino's** for an inaccessible website. Since we can't predict the future, anchor on a stable goal: **promoting human flourishing.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Harvard's Human Flourishing program** — five central domains:
> 1. **Happiness and life satisfaction**
> 2. **Mental and physical health**
> 3. **Meaning and purpose**
> 4. **Character and virtue**
> 5. **Close social relationships**

### Three questions to promote human flourishing
> [!EXAM]
> Ask of any feature you build:
> 1. Does my software **respect the humanity** of the users?
> 2. Does my software **amplify positive or negative behavior** for users and society?
> 3. Will my software's **quality impact the humanity** of others?

> [!INTUITION]
> Apply the three questions to a real feature — *"predict which students will attend events from their social-media activity."* It's efficient, but: does it **respect** users (or surveil them)? Does it **amplify** good behaviour (or manipulation)? What's the **privacy/quality impact**? The questions turn vague "is this ethical?" into a concrete checklist.
