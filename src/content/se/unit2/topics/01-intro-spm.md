# Introduction to Software Project Management

## What is a project? What is project management?

> [!NOTE]
> A **project** is a *temporary* effort to create **value** through a **unique** product, service or result. Every project has a **beginning and an end**, a **team**, a **budget**, a **schedule**, and a set of **expectations** to meet.

**Project management** is the use of specific knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to deliver something of value to people. **Software project management (SPM)** is the *"art and science of planning and leading software projects"* — a **sub-discipline** of general project management in which software projects are **planned, implemented, monitored and controlled**.

Software projects carry unique challenges: **changing requirements**, **technological complexity**, and the **intangible** nature of software (you can't see or touch it, so progress is hard to measure).

> [!INTUITION]
> Recall the **4 Ps** from Unit 1 — *People, Product, Process, Project*. SPM is the discipline of the fourth P: bringing People, Product and Process together under a plan. **People are the most important asset.**

## The Software Project Manager — three roles

The PM is the central figure responsible for project success, playing three roles:

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Leader** | Provides direction and vision; communicates goals and each member's responsibilities; **builds a diverse, skilled team**; handles motivation, conflict resolution and cohesion. |
| **Liaison** | The **communication bridge** between clients, the team, and upper management. Coordinates information flow and **translates technical concepts** for non-technical stakeholders. |
| **Mentor** | Guides the team through challenges, gives advice, fosters cohesion, supports professional growth. |

The PM also defines requirements, builds the team, lays out the project blueprint (scope + parameters), allots budget to tasks, and ensures stakeholder expectations are met on time.

> [!EXAM]
> Memorise the three roles: **Leader, Liaison, Mentor.** And the headline truth: *"Good project management cannot guarantee success, but **poor management always leads to failure**"* on significant projects.

## The Project Management Triangle (Triple Constraints)

The **project management triangle** visualises the *"triple constraints"* every project must balance to keep **quality** high:

| Constraint | Meaning | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | What gets delivered — features, functionality, quality standards | More scope → more time & cost |
| **Time** | Schedule and deadlines | Compressed time → more resources or less scope |
| **Cost** | Budget and resources | Overruns ← scope expansion or time compression |

> [!TRAP]
> **Quality sits at the *centre* of the triangle** — it is affected by every scope/time/cost decision. The key rule: **no single corner can change without trade-offs with the other two.** "Just add this feature" (scope ↑) always costs time, money, or quality.

*Example:* adding calendar integration to an event-management app (scope ↑) forces a choice — extend the timeline, raise the budget, drop other features, or accept lower quality. The PM's job is to communicate these trade-offs and balance all three.

## The SPM Lifecycle (5 phases)

| # | Phase | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Initiation** | Project definition, stakeholder identification, feasibility, project charter, initial scope |
| **2** | **Planning** | WBS, scheduling, resource allocation, risk-management planning |
| **3** | **Execution** | Implement planned activities, coordinate the team, create deliverables |
| **4** | **Monitoring & Control** | Track performance, identify issues, apply corrective action |
| **5** | **Closure** | Deliverable acceptance, lessons-learned, resource release |

> [!INTUITION]
> The **management** lifecycle *wraps* the **engineering** lifecycle. Picture the Project Management Life Cycle (Initiation → Planning → Monitoring/Execution → Closing) sitting **above** the SDLC (Requirements → Architecture → Design → Implementation → Testing → Deploy). The PM steers the project while engineers build the software inside it.
