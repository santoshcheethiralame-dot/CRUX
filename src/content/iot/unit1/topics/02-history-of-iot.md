# History & Genesis of IoT

The Internet of Things evolved in four broad waves: **early experiments → naming → technical maturity → mass adoption.**

## Early experiments (1980s–1990s)

Before anyone had a name for it, hobbyists and researchers wired everyday objects to the network:

- The famous **Carnegie Mellon Coke machine** (early 1980s) — students connected it to the ARPANET so they could check from their desks whether a bottle was cold and in stock. It is widely cited as the **first internet-connected appliance**.
- A networked **"smart" toaster** demoed at a 1990 conference, controllable over the internet.

## The term is coined (1999)

> [!NOTE]
> **Kevin Ashton** coined the phrase **"Internet of Things"** in **1999** while working at the **MIT Auto-ID Center**, in the context of using **RFID** tags to let supply-chain systems track physical goods automatically.

Ashton's key insight: computers of the era knew only what *humans typed into them*. If objects could **gather their own data** — without human data entry — we could track and count everything in the world, cutting waste and cost. RFID was the first practical way to give objects an identity the network could read.

> [!INTUITION]
> The "Auto-ID" name captures the original goal: **automatic identification** of physical things. RFID gave objects a *name*; sensors later gave them *senses*; connectivity gave them a *voice*.

## Technical maturity (2000s)

The enabling technologies matured and got cheap: low-cost **sensors**, **Wi-Fi/Bluetooth**, capable **microcontrollers**, and the first **cloud services**. This made it economical to connect ordinary products, not just research prototypes.

## Mass adoption (post-2010)

A rough timeline of the modern era:

| Period | What happened |
|---|---|
| **2010–2014** | Consumer IoT takes off — smart thermostats (**Nest**), smart bulbs, wearables. **AWS/Azure** begin offering IoT cloud services. |
| **2015–2018** | **Industrial IoT (IIoT)** gains traction — smart factories, predictive maintenance. Expansion into smart cities, healthcare, connected vehicles. |
| **2019–2022** | Growth of **Edge AI, TinyML, and 5G**. Security/privacy concerns drive regulation (**GDPR**, **ISO/IEC 30141** reference architecture). |
| **2023 →** | IoT market surpasses **$300+ billion**. Emerging trends: **Digital Twins, Blockchain-IoT, Green IoT**. |

## Where it's heading

- **Digital Twins** — live virtual replicas of physical assets for simulation and predictive maintenance.
- **Edge AI / TinyML** — running ML models *on the device* for instant, offline decisions.
- **Green IoT** — energy-harvesting, ultra-low-power designs to make billions of devices sustainable.
- **Blockchain-IoT** — tamper-evident device identity and data provenance.

> [!EXAM]
> Two facts are almost always asked: **Kevin Ashton coined "Internet of Things" in 1999 at MIT's Auto-ID Center**, and the original driver was **RFID-based supply-chain tracking**. Know the four-wave arc and at least one early example (the connected Coke machine).
