# Smart Cities, Healthcare, Agriculture & Connected Vehicles

## Smart Cities

> [!NOTE]
> A **smart city** uses technology and data collection — **ICT** and **IoT** — to improve quality of life and the sustainability and efficiency of city operations.

**Key components:**

- **Smart governance** — IoT-backed, data-driven urban planning (e.g., India's **Gati Shakti** master plan unifying infrastructure data across ministries).
- **Smart mobility & traffic** — adaptive signals from real-time congestion data, smart parking guidance, GPS-tracked public transport.
- **Smart environment** — air/noise pollution sensors, real-time water and waste management, disaster-forecasting weather sensors.

**Real-world examples:** Barcelona (smart parking, waste, adaptive lighting); Singapore (Smart Nation — citizen dashboards, predictive healthcare); Amsterdam (environmental & transport monitoring); Bhopal (smart poles, Integrated Command & Control Centre).

**Benefits:** better living standards, efficient resource use, less energy/water waste, safer and cleaner cities, more citizen engagement.

## Smart Healthcare (IoMT)

> [!NOTE]
> **Smart healthcare**, powered by the **Internet of Medical Things (IoMT)**, integrates IoT devices into healthcare for real-time patient monitoring, predictive diagnostics, and equipment management. **Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM)** is a key sub-domain.

**Key applications:**

- **Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM)** — wearables (smartwatches, ECG monitors, glucometers) track vitals (heart rate, SpO₂, temperature) and stream them to providers for chronic-illness management.
- **Predictive maintenance of medical equipment** — sensors on MRI machines, ventilators, infusion pumps predict faults before they cause care interruptions.
- **Medication adherence** — smart pill dispensers remind and track intake (valuable for elderly patients).
- **Asset tracking** — tags on wheelchairs, IV pumps, beds cut time spent locating equipment and prevent theft.

> [!INTUITION]
> IoMT's biggest shift is from **reactive to proactive care**. Instead of a patient noticing symptoms and visiting a hospital, continuous data streams catch anomalies *early*, enabling intervention before a condition escalates — fewer hospitalisations, lower cost, better outcomes.

## Smart Agriculture (Smart Farming)

> [!NOTE]
> **Smart farming** manages farms with modern ICT to increase the quantity and quality of produce while minimising human labour.

The technologies: **sensors** (soil, water, light, humidity, temperature), **connectivity** (cellular, LoRa), **location** (GPS, satellite), **robotics** (autonomous tractors), and **data analytics**.

**The smart-farming cycle (data → action loop):**

1. **Data collection** — field sensors gather soil/air/moisture data.
2. **Diagnostics** — the system analyses data and identifies problems.
3. **Decision-making** — software (or a human) decides what action to take.
4. **Action** — irrigation/fertilisation is performed; sensors re-measure and the cycle repeats.

**Applications:** precision crop monitoring, smart irrigation (water only when/where needed), drone surveillance (pest/health), livestock monitoring (wearable collars), automated machinery (GPS tractors), greenhouse automation.

## Connected Vehicles

> [!NOTE]
> A **connected vehicle** establishes wireless links with nearby devices and the internet (via WLAN), enabling **bi-directional communication** between vehicles and other vehicles, mobile devices, and infrastructure.

This is often called **V2X** ("vehicle-to-everything"), which includes **V2V** (vehicle-to-vehicle) and **V2I** (vehicle-to-infrastructure). Vehicles continuously broadcast their location and receive near-real-time information that triggers automated responses.

**Automotive IoT applications:** adaptive cruise control, real-time route planning and congestion avoidance, dynamic road-sign updates (weather/hazards), crash and wrong-way-driver alerts, automatic braking, and **semi-/fully autonomous driving**.

> [!INTUITION]
> The safety payoff of V2X is that cars see *around corners*. A car can be warned of a stopped vehicle or a wrong-way driver it cannot yet see, because that information arrives over the network (V2V/V2I) before line-of-sight — turning isolated cars into a cooperative, aware fleet.

> [!EXAM]
> For each vertical know: a **one-line definition**, **2–3 applications**, and the **core benefit**. High-frequency items: smart-city *components* (governance/mobility/environment), **IoMT = RPM**, the **4-step smart-farming loop**, and **V2X / V2V / V2I** for connected vehicles.
